1 In the month Nisan, in the twentieth year of Artaxerxes the king, when wine was before him, I picked up the wine, and gave it to the king. Now I had not been sad before in his presence.

2 The king said to me, “Why is your face sad, since you are not sick? This is nothing else but sorrow of heart.” Then I was very much afraid.

3 I said to the king, “Let the king live forever! Why shouldn’t my face be sad, when the city, the place of my fathers’ tombs, lies waste, and its gates have been consumed with fire?”

4 Then the king said to me, “What is your request?” So I prayed to the God of heaven.

King of Persia, 465–424 BC
⤷ In Nehemiah 2

He is the single point of failure in Nehemiah’s plan. He is also the king who had previously ordered the rebuilding of Jerusalem stopped by force (Ezra 4:17–23) — which is why asking him to reverse that is dangerous, not routine.

Artaxerxes I Longimanus, fifth king of the Achaemenid Empire and son of Xerxes I. He ruled from Susa, Persepolis and Babylon over territory reaching from the Indus to Egypt and the Aegean. His reign included the long Egyptian revolt (460–454 BC) and, by tradition, a settlement with Athens around 449 BC. He authorised both Ezra’s mission (458 BC) and Nehemiah’s.

○  Son of Xerxes I (the king of Esther)

Understand this passage

Nehemiah 2 · 445 BC | Persian Empire | Jerusalem

Around 140 years after Jerusalem was destroyed. Nehemiah is a Jewish official serving the Persian king. Ezra has already returned, but Jerusalem's walls remain broken.

Who

Nehemiah, the Jewish royal cupbearer, and Artaxerxes I, ruler of Persia.

Where

The Persian royal court, traditionally associated with Susa; Jerusalem is the destination.

When

Around 445/444 BC, during the Persian period after the exile.

What

The king notices Nehemiah's sadness, and Nehemiah asks to rebuild Jerusalem.

Before

Nehemiah learned that Jerusalem's walls remained broken, then mourned, fasted, and prayed.

Stakes

He needs royal authorization, safe passage, and resources for a politically sensitive project.
